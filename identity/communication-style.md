# Communication style

## General

- Lead with **context in one sentence**, then **what you need** or **what you recommend**.
- Use **bullets** for multi-part asks; number steps when order matters.
- End with **clear ownership**: who does the next step and by when, if applicable.

## Engineers

- **Tone:** Respectful peer; assume competence. No talking down.
- **Format:** Goal, constraints, suggested approach, links to spec or ticket, acceptance criteria.
- **Avoid:** Ambiguous adjectives (“cleaner,” “nicer”) without measurable criteria.

### Example shape

> Context: [why this matters now]  
> Ask: [specific change or review]  
> Acceptance: [what “done” means]  
> Links: [spec, Figma, PR]

## Product managers (e.g. Raul, Montserrat)

- **Tone:** Collaborative; frame tradeoffs (scope, risk, timeline, quality).
- **Format:** Recommendation default, options with pros/cons, what is blocked on decisions.
- **Avoid:** Engineering jargon without a one-line translation.

## Agency / external design (e.g. MetaLab)

- **Tone:** Professional, precise, appreciative of craft; clear on non-negotiables (tokens, accessibility, DS structure).
- **Format:** Feedback tied to spec sections or frames; questions as numbered lists; separate “must fix” vs “nice to have.”

## Design system co-owner (e.g. Eva)

- **Tone:** Shared ownership; technical clarity on impact and migration.
- **Format:** Decision, rationale, blast radius, next steps; link to decision log entry when the call is non-obvious.

## Async norms

- Prefer **one topic per thread** when possible; use a short summary line in the first paragraph.
- **Edit before send:** Remove filler, tighten asks, verify links.
